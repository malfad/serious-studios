When we take on a brand identity project, the first question we ask isn't "what colors?" It's "what does this brand sound like?"

Typography is voice. It's the one design element that communicates even before the reader consciously processes it.

## The wrong way to pick a typeface

Most designers open their font browser and scroll until something looks right. This is completely backwards. Type selection should come after a thorough understanding of the brand's personality, audience, and competitive landscape.

A fintech startup needs to signal trust and modernity simultaneously — very different requirements than a luxury skincare brand that needs warmth and exclusivity. Same category of geometric sans-serif, completely different execution.

## What we look for

**Weight range.** A typeface with a wide weight range gives you expressive flexibility. You can set a headline at 800 weight and body copy at 300 and still feel cohesive.

**Optical size behavior.** Some typefaces fall apart at small sizes. If your brand lives on mobile, this matters enormously.

**Personality at display sizes.** Pull your chosen typeface up to 120px. Does it have character? Does it say something? That's how it'll appear on billboards, hero banners, and print.

## The pairing problem

The conventional advice is "pair a serif with a sans-serif." This is lazy. Good type pairings come from contrast of personality, not just category.

We often pair two fonts from the same broad category — two grotesques, for instance — but use them for entirely different roles: one exclusively for display, one for body. The harmony comes from the shared skeleton; the contrast comes from their distinct voices.
