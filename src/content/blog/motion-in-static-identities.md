A brand identity used to live primarily on paper. Then on screens. Now, increasingly, in motion — on social platforms where static posts are disadvantaged by the algorithm, in presentations that embed video, on websites where even a logo might animate on load.

Most brand identities weren't designed for this. They were designed for print, adapted for screen, and then stretched (often badly) into motion.

## Why motion is a brand character issue, not a production issue

The most common mistake: treating motion as something you do *to* a finished identity rather than something you design *into* it from the start.

When motion is an afterthought, you get logos that spin, bounce, or fade in a way that has no relationship to the brand's character. The animation feels generic because it is — it could belong to anyone.

When motion is designed in, you ask different questions at the identity stage: How does this brand enter a space? Does it arrive deliberately or spontaneously? Does it move with weight or lightness? Is it patient or urgent?

These aren't production questions. They're personality questions. And the answers should be discoverable in the static identity if it was designed right.

## The motion principles framework

We now deliver a Motion Principles section as part of every identity system. It covers:

**Easing character** — does this brand move with linear momentum, ease in/out, or an elastic snap? Each communicates something different. A financial brand that bounces feels wrong. A youth brand that moves linearly feels stiff.

**Entry and exit behavior** — how does the brand arrive? A reveal that builds from a detail to the whole communicates something different from a logo that drops in at full scale.

**Duration sensibility** — fast brands are confident. Slow brands are considered. Neither is correct in the abstract; the right duration depends on context and platform.

**What doesn't move** — as important as what does. Identifying the elements that should always be stable gives the animated elements contrast and meaning.

## Practical constraints

Motion guidelines that can only be executed by a senior motion designer are useless to most brand teams. We design for the 80% use case: social content created by a content manager with After Effects and one hour.

This means prebuilt templates, simple timing rules, and a small set of pre-approved transition behaviors. The complex, fully realized motion pieces — brand films, product launches — are a separate track with separate briefs.

The goal is consistency at scale, not perfection in isolation.
