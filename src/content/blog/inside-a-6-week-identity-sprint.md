Six weeks is short for a brand identity. It's also, for the right brief and the right client relationship, enough — if the process is tight and the decisions are made at the right pace.

Here's what those six weeks actually look like.

## Week 1: Listening

We don't design anything in week one. We talk, read, look, and listen.

The kickoff meeting isn't a brief walk-through. It's a structured session where we ask the founders or brand leads to describe the brand in terms of things it's not. What are the three brands in your category you most want to be distinguished from? What word would make you cringe if a journalist used it to describe you?

Negative space is often clearer than positive territory. People know what they don't want. We start there.

We also do our own research: competitive landscape audit, audience context, cultural moment the brand is entering. By the end of week one, we have a synthesis document — one page — that captures what we've heard. We share it with the client before touching a pencil.

## Week 2: Direction setting

We present three Brand Direction Territories (not mood boards — see our earlier post on why). Each one is a different strategic position: a different answer to "what should this brand be known for?"

The territories are written, not visual. Each is a short paragraph describing the brand's character, one or two reference points that embody the feeling (from outside the category), and the core design implication.

We pick one direction together and document the decision. This is the most important meeting of the project.

## Week 3–4: Concept development

With direction locked, we design. In a typical sprint we develop two logomark directions, each with a rationale grounded in the brand character decisions from week two.

We present these as systems, not isolated marks: color applied, in use on a surface, at small scale, alongside a typeface direction. Never on a white background with a drop shadow.

The client selects a direction, gives consolidated feedback, and we move to refinement. One round of revisions within this two-week block.

## Week 5: System build

With the primary mark refined and approved, we build the surrounding system: typeface selection and pairing, full color palette, secondary graphic elements if they're part of the system, and the core brand voice principles.

We also deliver the first applications: business card, social profile, one printed piece. Not to show off — to test the system in actual use before guidelines are written.

## Week 6: Delivery

Final files, brand guidelines (PDF, maximum 40 pages — if it's longer, it won't be read), and a 90-minute handover call where we walk through the logic of every decision.

We insist on the handover call. Files without context produce misapplication. The guidelines are a reference; the call is the education.

Six weeks after kickoff, the client has a complete brand identity with the rationale to defend every decision. That's the deliverable. The files are just how it travels.
